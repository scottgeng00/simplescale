You are planning a collection of alternative web documents derived from one source document.

Generate exactly {n} distinct prompts for another model. That model will receive each prompt together with the original source document and use it to write one new document.

Your task is to write the prompts, not the resulting documents. Each prompt must work independently of the other prompts.

### Objective

Design prompts that produce a diverse collection of plausible web text grounded in the source. Each resulting document should have a recognizable purpose and a natural reason to exist: explaining something, answering a question, documenting a process, comparing alternatives, discussing a disagreement, recording notes, making an argument, or another purpose supported by the source.

The collection should present the source's information through meaningfully different perspectives, organizations, levels of detail, and styles of writing. Educational explanations are one possibility among many.

### Step 1: divide the content before writing prompts

Before writing any prompt, work out (in your reasoning) the source's distinct content units: its separate claims, examples, anecdotes, numbers, procedures, people, distinctions, and qualifications. Then give each prompt a different primary focus.

- No two prompts may be built around the same anecdote, example, or main point. If two prompts would end up making the same central point in different packaging, change one of them.
- At most two prompts may be broad treatments covering most of the source. The others should each center on a different slice, or connect information from different parts of the source in a way no other prompt does.
- Spread attention so that less obvious details and later parts of the source are represented, not only the opening or the most striking point.
- A focused prompt must keep the qualifications and context needed for its slice to stay faithful.

### Step 2: choose a distinct design for each prompt

For each prompt choose a combination of communicative purpose, organizing principle, web form, audience and assumed familiarity, voice, and level of polish. Every prompt in the collection must differ from every other in purpose and form, not only in tone or title.

To keep collections from different sources from all looking alike, this request comes with a randomly drawn menu. Prefer forms and organizing principles from it when they genuinely suit the source and its slice; skip any that do not fit, and use something else if a better choice exists. Never force a form that would require content the source does not have (for example, a timeline for a source with no dates, or a review of something the source does not evaluate).

Each prompt also has an assigned length and writing quality. Respect them, choosing a form and slice that fit the assigned length (a very short assignment suits a comment, reply, brief answer, short notes, or a summary blurb; a very long one suits a thorough guide, reference page, or deep dive). If the source is too thin for a long assignment without padding or invention, cover the source thoroughly at the length it supports.

### Writing quality

Include the range of writing quality found on the web, following the assignments above. Rough writing may be meandering, repetitive, abrupt, unevenly organized, overly verbose, terse, awkwardly phrased, or fragmentary, with generic openings, clumsy headings, or inconsistent detail. Choose imperfections that make sense for the particular author, purpose, and web context. Random spelling errors alone are not a meaningful quality variation.

### Fidelity rules (put the relevant ones into each prompt)

- Presentation may vary, but source-derived claims, numbers, conditions, attributions, time spans, and uncertainty must stay as the source states them. Do not strengthen or soften stances; keep opinions, interpretations, and disputed claims marked as such.
- Illustrative scenarios and examples are fine when they are clearly framed as hypothetical. Never ask for invented quotations, citations, statistics, studies, events, discoveries, or personal experiences presented as real (no "I recently visited...", "a reader who examined..."). An author may react, give an opinion, ask a question, or say how the information relates to them in general terms.
- Do not ask for invented metadata: no dates, times, bylines, real-sounding names of authors or organizations, ticket numbers, prices, or contact details that the source does not contain. Forum or comment participants may use plain usernames.
- Do not ask for limitations, caveats, or unanswered questions that the source does not itself state or clearly imply.
- If the source is mostly questions, a worksheet, a list, or a fragment, do not ask the writer to supply missing answers or facts; build documents around what is actually there.

### How to write each prompt

Address each prompt directly to the writing model. Give it enough direction to establish:

- What document to write and why that document would exist.
- Which specific source material to use and emphasize, named concretely (concepts, claims, examples, entities, numbers, qualifications). Simply mentioning the source's topic in an otherwise generic prompt is insufficient; the source-specific details should determine how the document is organized.
- How to organize or transform that material.
- The audience, voice, assigned quality, and assigned length.
- The fidelity constraints that matter most for that transformation.

Vary how the prompts themselves are phrased: do not begin them all the same way (avoid starting most prompts with "Write a"), and do not follow one fixed template.

Refer to the material by what it is (for instance "the review", "the clinic's guidelines", "the recipe", "the council report"), never as "the source", "the provided text", or "the document"; the finished documents must stand on their own without commentary about any rewriting task. Every finished document is plain web text: no Markdown formatting, no HTML. In every prompt, ask for faithfulness to the material's meaning and qualifications, and ask for only the finished document to be returned.

### Output format

Return exactly {n} prompts, in the order of the assignments above. Prefix each prompt with a Markdown heading beginning with `##`, followed by a short descriptive title. Beneath the heading, provide only the executable prompt. Do not include an introduction, explanations of your choices, source summaries, or any of the rewritten documents.
